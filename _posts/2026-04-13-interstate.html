--- title: 'Interstate 676' date: 2026-04-13 categories: poetry excerpt: For the victims of the June 1st, 2020 attack by police on protesters on I-676 in Philadelphia. image: /assets/images/interstate/676gas.png teaser: /assets/images/interstate/676gas.png ---

A first-person perspective of the attack, filmed by the independent videographer (and victim of the attack) Sunni Singh.

* * *

I see Mary in the manger —
her body draped in blue —
Joseph's arm around her,
craning his neck to view
the immaculate life:
You.
The son who'd die
for the sins of all.

And I see myself in them
(a version that always falls short)
barren without womb,
martyr without sacrifice.
Her wails bastardize in our lungs,
choking on tear gas
with blood baptizing the grass
and tears watering the roots.

And I feel God's earth as I crawl
through the screaming forest
with hairy tree trunks
and roots of rubber.
I imagine the logger, cowering
with his axe, in fear of a plant
that he finally deems
alive.

But what remains for us
humans without disguise?
To transcend is to devolve,
and to evolve is to die.
And so I rise to my feet;
as a bullet cracks my skull,
strangers call for their mothers
and the good God above.

* * *

Gwenyth Galloway is a poet and writer native to Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Her work explores topics including environmentalism, interpersonal relationships, and the rise of contemporary American fascism.
