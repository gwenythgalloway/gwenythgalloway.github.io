♫ Put away basketball / go to work / in calcium mine ♫

I often wonder how much my perception of cisgender, heterosexual female desire has been determined by the circumstances of my existence: an unmistakably queer "f*ggot" turned transsexual. Are the women most likely to have me as a friend also the most likely to desire a "progressive" man? In any case, my interpersonal friendships and social media trawling have presented a common theme: a man who is, by all accounts, "modern" in his behavior - "metrosexual", as some call it. He uses a body wash that's meant purely for the body. He cleanses his hair with a shampoo that doesn't come with conditioner already in it - let alone, god forbid, a 3-in-1 that lacks conditioner entirely. He has a skincare routine. He has a favorite moisturizer and sunscreen. He doesn't believe that either are a threat to his testicular volume or testosterone production. He probably even says that physical touch is his preferred "love language".

"Babe, my therapist told me that you not sucking my dick daily is preventing me from developing secure attachment. By the way, do you have your half of the rent money?”

One of the first times I ever witnessed my “type” in media was with the “Warsaw Globetrotters”, a humorous parody on Family Guy of the famous Harlem Globetrotters basketball team. The premise is simple: three nonchalant Polish men, with stubble rough enough to sand wood to a silky grain, dressed in stained garments filthy with oil and grease, cut their performance short to return to the calcium mine. They have chest hair poking out of their tank tops, and functional guts holding some combination of muscle and visceral vodka fat. A homely woman looks on from the stands, donned in an apron and with a scarf on her head, looking ready for the backbreaking toils of the factory or farm. The gym around them is crumbling, covered in grime and dirt, with bricks falling out of the walls.

It’s the aesthetic that has come to stereotype the former Soviet bloc : an aesthetic reminiscent of one’s ancestors from a genealogy deep-dive — of one’s great-great-grandmother who birthed 14 children over the course of her lifetime. It’s an aesthetic borne of abject working-class poverty, of hard industrial and agricultural labor, and of the political right’s wet dream of “RETVRNing” to tradition. It is, in essence, an aesthetic of raw material, of earnest attempts at maximizing potential, and of a firm divide between the social, political, and economic spheres of women and men. Why, then, would a modern transsexual woman find herself longing for a man who evokes an era that is seemingly incompatible with her existence?

My friends alternate between some combination of chastisement, bewilderment, and genuine concern for my well-being. The theme has been one and the same since my youth — from my early days of being told that “Team Edward” was vastly superior to “Team Jacob”, to my teenage years of my friends continuing to fangirl over vampiric twinks while I prayed for a Neanderthalic lumberjack to come to life, drag me to his (self-constructed) cabin in the woods, and allow me to tend to my dream garden of garlic and herbs. The insinuation has been clear: that I’m “regressive”, wrestling with “internalized misogyny and femphobia”, and sabotaging my prospects of having a fulfilling relationship. How is a caveman supposed to be emotionally fulfilling and intelligent? How am I supposed to be happy with a man who communicates in grunts and grumbles?

To my friends: I offer you a one-up and a diversion. A viral social media post by transsexual music star Ethel Cain (pictured above), posted in reference to her 2025 album Perverts, details her sexual longing for the Bruce Mansfield Power Plant in Shippingport, PA. At least a caveman - a Homo sapiens - is human under the popular definition. At least a caveman can communicate in some capacity, even if it predates advanced linguistic communication. Yet, as humorous as I find it to be upon first glance, the more I sit with it, the more I realize that it isn't "extreme" or alien in comparison to my tastes - it's my own taste embodied in something ostensibly inhuman. The power plant speaks in its own form of grunts and grumbles: it speaks in pulses of energy and steam. Much like the Polish miner, it reads to me as (hyper)masculinity, as "traditional masculinity", and as power - literal power, in this instance. And for Ethel Cain, a straight transsexual woman with an alleged affinity for Marlboro-smelling roughnecks (half-looking at you, Jack Donoghue), I don't believe that's coincidental. But why does the power plant read as "traditionally masculine"?
